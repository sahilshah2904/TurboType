# Friends multiplayer rooms — free-only plan

Status: designed 22 October 2026 at the user's request; **not implemented, provisioned or deployed**. This is Phase 11 after the solo launch, using Phase 9 identity/save foundations. It supersedes unspecified room limits, email-OTP-first sign-in and paid-server assumptions in older notes. Existing solo gameplay is unchanged.

## Scope and budget

- Private fun for up to 100 approved friends/relatives. No public matchmaking, paid features, chat, voice, uploads or spectators in the first multiplayer release.
- A room has **2–10 human racers, including the host**. Every player selects a car; duplicate car models are allowed. No CPU fill in this first version.
- Interpret the user's 10-at-a-time limit conservatively: launch admission permits **at most 10 active racers across the project**, with room size also capped at 10. Smaller rooms may coexist only within that total. A full launch budget shows a retry message; increasing concurrency requires a new measured free-tier budget, not a paid upgrade.
- **Hard cash budget: $0.** Use genuinely free plans, default provider domains and no payment method, paid add-ons, trial-to-paid dependencies, SMS, paid email, dedicated server or custom domain. Do not turn on overage billing or automatically upgrade. Recheck terms before provisioning. If a required capability cannot fit a free service, reduce/defer online scope and retain solo play.
- The shared game URL remains on GitHub Pages. Multiplayer is an explicit Hub choice with **Create room** and **Join room**, not an automatic online connection on every game launch. Keep new buttons out of playable navigation until functional; the cinematic opening/tutorial is still Phase 6.

## Create, join and ready

1. A signed-in, approved player clicks **Create room**, enters a short room name and becomes host. Backend generates a collision-checked, cryptographically random **8-character room code** using a readable alphabet without ambiguous characters. Display Copy code and an optional invitation URL; both resolve to the same private lobby. A room code is an invitation lookup, not a login credential.
2. Friends choose **Join room**, enter that code (case/spacing normalized), and join if signed in, approved, not banned from that room, and a slot is available. Join and slot reservation are transactional: an eleventh player cannot slip in during a race between requests. One membership/active driving session per account; extra tabs cannot consume extra player slots.
3. Host chooses a track **they own**, plus a shared multiplayer passage/difficulty preset and time cap (proposed default 120 seconds; presets bounded to 60–180). Everyone sees the same passage/rules/deadline. Track choice is cosmetic and independent of passage difficulty. These are private multiplayer exercises, not campaign node clears.
4. Each player selects one of **their own** starter/earned cars, chooses a readable display name and clicks **I'm ready** after required assets and rules load. Display the roster, host badge, car, connection and Ready/Not ready status. The host must also click I'm ready.
5. Host can click **Start race** only with at least two connected members and **every current member ready** for the same configuration/roster version. Enforce this in the backend as well as the UI. No force-start over an unready player and no automatic start.
6. Backend freezes roster, car choices, track, passage revision, rules version and match ID, reserves the global active-player capacity atomically and publishes a future server start timestamp for a shared **3–2–1–GO**. A missed preload/disconnect before GO cancels back to lobby with readiness cleared rather than producing a false start.

Changing host track/passage/deadline or changing the roster clears everyone's readiness. Changing a car clears that player's readiness. Host can remove a player before countdown, regenerate the invite code or close the room; a removed account cannot immediately rejoin using the same code. Players can leave or unready freely in the lobby. Validate/escape names; never render supplied HTML. Server versions reject stale or duplicate Start/Ready/configuration messages.

**Track access:** guests in a room may race on the host's owned track for that match even if they have not earned it. This is temporary room access, not an unlock or a saved entitlement. Car selection remains per-player ownership. The temporary track grant survives host departure once a race is frozen; a later lobby host must own the selected track or it falls back to a starter, clearing readiness. All assets are original and supplied by the game; only availability is permissioned.

## Race, results and room lifecycle

- Online time never pauses for one player. Leaving the tab, opening Settings or disconnecting cannot freeze opponents. Local controls may mute/simplify rendering; returning restores a server snapshot.
- Keep the responsive typing engine local, with immediate feedback; network requests never block a keystroke. The backend validates bounded sequenced input/progress batches against the frozen passage and server clock, rejects impossible/duplicate/out-of-order claims and owns accepted finishes/results. Do not equate Broadcast delivery with score validation. Browser automation cannot be eliminated; these are private friend results, not a cheat-proof ranked service.
- Provisional positions interpolate between accepted updates. Final placement uses verified finish receipt times relative to the shared server start, with a documented tie policy and DNF at the common deadline. Flush pending input in the finish request immediately; do not wait for the next periodic batch. Do not trust a client-submitted WPM, elapsed time or finish flag as authority. Validate the detailed batching/latency policy in TT-1102 before release.
- Reconnect grace: proposed **30 seconds**, with the race clock continuing. Rejoin the same authenticated slot and match; show Reconnecting and a stale-position marker. Do not invent typed progress during the gap. Lost/unacknowledged input is recovered only through bounded validated replay; otherwise resume at the last accepted position with an explicit notice. After grace, DNF; later returns can view results but cannot re-enter that race.
- Lobby host disappearance: reserve their slot for the reconnect grace, then transfer host to the longest-connected remaining player and reset readiness. During a race, host loss never stops other racers; the backend continues to own race state and a new lobby host is selected. If everyone leaves, expire the room rather than retaining a zombie session.
- Room idle expiry: proposed **30 minutes**; clear abandoned rooms and membership/ban records within **24 hours**. Server timestamps, expiring capacity leases and lazy checks on backend requests must make expiry work without an always-running paid process. Cleanup scheduling must also remain free. A total backend outage marks the race interrupted/unverified; never fabricate winners or offline online rewards.
- Results show all entrants, actual final status, WPM, accuracy and time; include unfinished/disconnected players. Return to the same lobby for Rematch; clear ready flags and obtain fresh readiness before host starts again. New code is not needed unless the host regenerates it or the room expires.
- Multiplayer does **not** create campaign clears, stars or car/track milestone progress in the first release. Keep friend match history separate from solo records and imported guest data. The existing independent unlock schedules remain cars 15/30/45/60/75/90 and tracks 10/25/40/55/70/85/100 within each category; owned cosmetics are globally usable on already-unlocked solo levels.
- The current five-racer renderer is not proof of a ten-player layout. Design/test a readable ten-entrant race/standings presentation at supported laptop widths, keeping the typing strip, name/position information and results usable. Preserve Low effects, Reduced motion, large text and keyboard access.

## Preferred free architecture, subject to prototype gates

**Candidate: GitHub Pages + one Supabase Free project** for Auth, Postgres, access policies and private Realtime. Do not add Colyseus/another host merely because older research named it. Supabase's suitability for validated ten-player races must be demonstrated before final provider selection or account/project provisioning; nothing is connected today.

- Static game/art/audio stays on Pages. Backend holds approved profiles, solo clear receipts/independent entitlements, room state, active match slots and bounded finalized match summaries.
- Prefer provider-hosted **Google OAuth** through Supabase, with an owner-approved account allowlist, so TurboType never collects a raw password and does not depend on email/SMS delivery. Confirm OAuth configuration/publishing and family-account suitability before adoption. Do not require a GitHub account to play. Keep local solo guest play; require a persistent approved identity for multiplayer and cross-device saves. Use provider-supported recovery; no home-built password store.
- Built-in Supabase email delivery is limited and is **not** a scalable free invitation/login service. Do not quietly add paid SMTP if OAuth cannot be configured. Show a clear account/setup blocker and revisit a verified free alternative.
- All writes affecting room membership, host permissions, ready/start state, progress, results and ownership go through validated backend operations. Use row-level policies and private-channel membership authorization. A public frontend key grants no administrative rights; service credentials remain outside GitHub/browser code. Invite codes are rate-limited (proposed five failed joins per account/minute with cooldown), unlisted and never a substitute for authorization.
- Supabase Realtime is transport, not the authoritative game server. Prototype transactional database RPCs/constraints and bounded validators for this non-physics typing game. Reuse the pure rule semantics with parity tests. If server functions are required, include their separate invocation limits in the budget. Never deploy an unvalidated client-hosted authority as a free substitute.
- Tables/contracts to design: approved profiles, category-qualified clear receipts, kind/category/threshold reward receipts, rooms/configuration version, room members/readiness/car/host lease, matches/frozen configuration, per-player sequence/accepted progress/finish, finalized results and usage/capacity leases. IDs and mutations are idempotent; timestamps come from the backend. An ordinary host is a lobby coordinator, not a database administrator.

## Verified free-tier snapshot and usage design

Checked against official documentation on **22 October 2026**; these are current provider limits, not a promise they will remain unchanged:

| Service | Relevant free limit / behavior | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GitHub Pages | Published site ≤1 GB; soft bandwidth 100 GB/month; Free Pages uses a public repository | Static delivery only; optimized assets and no sensitive credentials/data in the repo. [Limits](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits) |
| Supabase Free | 500 MB database, 5 GB egress, 50,000 monthly active users; two active free projects; idle projects may pause after a week | One small project, up to 100 approved profiles, compact saves, no art/audio served from the backend. Owner can restore a sleeping project before a family session. [Pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), [pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing) |
| Realtime Free | 200 concurrent connections; 2 million messages/month | Ten players fit the connection ceiling, but messages/fan-out still need budgeting. [Pricing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/pricing) |
| Realtime throughput | 100 messages/second; Presence limited separately | Do not broadcast every keystroke or frame. [Limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits) |
| Auth email | Built-in provider: two emails/hour | Prefer configured social login; no dependence on batch email invitations. [Auth limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits), [Google sign-in](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login/auth-google) |

**Proposed transport budget:** input batching up to once/second per active player plus immediate final submission; coalesce backend-validated room state into **one shared snapshot/second per active room**, not one ten-recipient broadcast for every player's update. Only publish changed state. Locally animate smoothly without pretending interpolated finishes are final. Coalesce concurrent finishes/ready changes too; acknowledgements can be individual responses rather than ten competing room broadcasts. Use low-frequency liveness checks and presence only for membership, not typing progress. Connect only while in an online lobby/race; unsubscribe on exit and reuse one client connection.

Illustrative calculation, **not a load-test result or guaranteed monthly capacity**: a 180-second race with ten subscribers and one room Broadcast per second costs about (1 send + 10 deliveries) × 180 = **1,980 messages** for progress snapshots. Ten such races/day for 30 days is **594,000 messages**, before joins, controls, presence, reconnects, database notifications and other sessions. At a 2 KB snapshot, that traffic is roughly **1.2 GB** before protocol/other traffic. Five small two-player rooms may have more total per-room overhead than one full room; budget that topology separately. [How messages are counted](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/realtime-messages).

Start with an internal whole-project budget of **1 million realtime messages/month, 3 GB backend egress/month and 100 MB application data**, leaving provider headroom. Meter admission and conservative estimated fan-out server-side; compare against provider usage reports because estimates omit overhead and shared-project traffic. At the app budget or provider restriction, refuse new online races, explain capacity/reset/recovery and keep local solo usable. Account/cloud saves also need clear pending/offline states; do not claim they synced during a pause. No paid fallback. Excess quota can restrict service; it does not justify an upgrade. [Billing FAQ](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-faq).

Keep only bounded summaries (proposed last 20 friend results per account within 30 days), accepted campaign receipts and current profile state. Prune abandoned rooms and ephemeral input batches; do not store every key indefinitely or send art/audio through Realtime. Preserve entitlement/clear receipts and bounded comparable personal-best/aggregate friend statistics independently of removable match history. Provide manual backup/export and restore instructions within free capabilities; do not assume paid managed backups or uptime guarantees. No artificial keep-alive jobs merely to defeat free-project pausing.
